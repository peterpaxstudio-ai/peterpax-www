AI and Google reviews: what changed in replying to them

By Piotr Gancarz Published 24 November 2026 2 min read

This year Google started testing AI-generated suggested replies to reviews, and in June it announced a connection between Gemini and your business profile. For a small business owner this means a faster draft reply, but you still decide what goes out publicly. Below is what is known and what is not yet known.

What changed this year

  • A test of suggested replies in the business dashboard (March 2026). According to a Search Engine Roundtable report from 24 March 2026, Google was testing an option to reply to reviews with AI in the Business Profile dashboard. It suggested a reply to an older review. Only some businesses saw it, and the report rests on a single screenshot, not an official announcement.
  • Gemini connected to your profile (June 2026). According to a report from 13 June 2026, Google announced on 10 June that Gemini, once connected to a profile, can read reviews, customer questions and performance data. On request it can prepare a reply to a specific review in the business’s tone. The rollout was due to start worldwide in June, initially for businesses with a single profile.

What this gives a small business

  • A faster first draft of a reply instead of writing from scratch.
  • A reply that refers to what the review actually says, rather than one canned template.
  • Fewer excuses not to reply regularly, since reviews without answers make a profile look abandoned.

What to watch for

  • Not everything is confirmed. The March test is described by independent observers, and in the report about Gemini the author notes it is unclear whether replies are only drafts or can be published. Check availability and rules in your own account.
  • Data and privacy. The same report notes that Google has not yet explained how profile data is retained in Gemini app activity.
  • Reply quality depends on your data. If the information in your profile is incomplete or inconsistent, the draft will repeat it.
  • A negative review is not a template. For a client complaint, always read the AI draft and edit it yourself.
  • Replying is only half the job. The other half is getting new reviews from clients who rarely think of it themselves.

What to do about it

If you reply to a few reviews a month, Google’s own tool and your attention are enough. If you get many reviews, run several locations, or want someone to regularly ask clients for a review after a visit, you need a process, not just suggestions.
