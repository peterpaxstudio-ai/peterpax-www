Gmail with Gemini: what AI in the inbox means for a small firm

By Piotr Gancarz Published 18 November 2026 2 min read

Google added Gemini-based features to Gmail that summarize threads, suggest replies and arrange your inbox by importance. They save real time on everyday email, but a general assistant does not know how your business works. Below is what Google announced and what to watch for.

What Google announced

According to Google’s official post from 8 January 2026, the new features include:

  • AI Overviews in threads: summaries of long conversations. Google says they are available to all users at no cost.
  • Asking your inbox questions: you can ask Gmail, for example, to find renovation quotes from a year ago. This needs a paid Google AI Pro or Ultra plan.
  • Help me write and suggested replies: available at no cost.
  • Proofreading: for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers.
  • AI Inbox: a new view that shows a summary of your most important matters and tasks instead of a chronological list. Availability depends on your plan and country.

At launch the features reached users in the US, in English. Google announced more languages and regions, so check in your own account whether they are available yet.

What this changes in a small business

  • You read a long thread faster, because you get a short summary.
  • Simple client questions come with a draft reply to edit.
  • You find an old email by asking a question instead of browsing folders (this feature is paid).

What these features will not solve

  • Your own processes. A general assistant does not know which matters are urgent, who handles them and what answer you give a client. A letter from a tax office and a newsletter are just two messages to it.
  • Other mailboxes. These are Gmail features. If your business email lives elsewhere, nothing changes.
  • Privacy by default. The more email goes to an AI model, the more important it is to check consciously what you switch on and on what terms.

What to do about it

Start with the free features and see for a week whether they save you time. If your business inbox gets dozens or hundreds of messages a day, including letters from offices and client enquiries, you need rules tailored to your business, not just general summaries.
